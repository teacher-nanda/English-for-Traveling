Travel tips

Nine mistakes tourists make on public transport

What locals do differently

Public transport is the cheapest and often the fastest way to see a city, but tourists often make the same mistakes. The first is buying the wrong ticket. Many cities have tickets that cover the subway, bus, and tram for a certain time. In London, for example, paying with a contactless card is much cheaper than buying a paper ticket with cash.

A second mistake is forgetting to validate a paper ticket. On many systems you must stamp the ticket before you travel, and inspectors can give you an on-the-spot fine even if the mistake was an accident. Tapping a card or phone at the gates avoids this problem. Daily or weekly passes can also save money if you travel a lot.

Direction is another problem. Station signs usually show the name of the last stop on the line, not words like uptown or downtown, and some lines split into branches. Check the map in the station before you board. Use an app such as Google Maps, Citymapper, or the city's official app, and keep a paper map in case your phone dies.

Experts also warn about timing. Many metros close at night, and night buses are less frequent, so check the last departure. Keep a little local cash for places that do not take cards, and remember that pickpockets like busy trains, escalators, and gates. Keep your bag in front of you.

Finally, be a good passenger. Stand on the right side of escalators, step aside when you need to look at a map, and let people get off the train before you get on.

Sources: Rick Steves and AOL
