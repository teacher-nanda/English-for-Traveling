📐 Grammar

How phrasal verbs work

A phrasal verb is a verb + a small word (off, up, in, out).
The meaning is often different from the verb alone.
Some phrasal verbs can be separated, and some cannot.

Verbdrop
Objectme
Small wordoff

Separable

NounPlease fill out the form.
Please fill out the form.
Both are correct.
PronounPlease fill it out.
Never: fill out it.

Inseparable

Always togetherWe set off early.
The plane took off.
I checked in online.
With an objectWe went through security.
I'm looking for my gate.

Travel tips

How early should you get to the airport?

Advice from travel experts on arriving in time

For most U.S. travelers, the standard advice is simple: arrive about two hours before a domestic flight and about three hours before an international one. International flights take longer because there are extra document checks and the terminals are often larger.

But the right time depends on the situation. Large airports with long walks to the gates, busy travel periods such as summer and the holidays, and checking a bag can all add time. Standing in line to check a bag can sometimes take up to an hour.

Airlines also set hard deadlines. Check-in usually closes about thirty minutes before a domestic flight and sixty minutes before an international one, and bag drop closes about forty-five minutes before a domestic flight. If you miss them, the airline can refuse to check you in, even if your plane is still at the gate.

There are ways to save time. Checking in online and traveling with only a carry-on lets you go straight to security. TSA PreCheck allows you to keep your shoes, laptop, and liquids in your bag, and CLEAR speeds up the identity check. Used together, they can cut the time you need to about forty-five minutes at some airports.

Coming home can be the most time-consuming part. When you return to the United States, you may go through passport checks more than once, so experts suggest giving yourself extra time.

Sources: TripIt and AAA
